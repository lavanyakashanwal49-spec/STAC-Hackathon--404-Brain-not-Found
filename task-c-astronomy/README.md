

### Objective

Analyse relative-brightness observations of three stars—**STAC-1, STAC-2, and STAC-3**—to identify possible transiting exoplanets, estimate their properties, and select a candidate for further habitability investigation.

### Methodology

The analysis uses:
- The transit method to identify possible exoplanets.
- Transit depth to estimate planetary radius.
- Transit timing to estimate orbital period.
- Kepler's third law to estimate orbital distance.
- A simplified habitable-zone distance to compare potential habitability.

The calculations use the assumptions specified in the report, including circular orbits and central transits.

### Results

| Candidate | Estimated Period | Transit Depth | Estimated Radius | Classification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| STAC-1 b | 4.25 days | 0.30% | 5.98 Earth radii | Neptune-sized |
| STAC-2 b | 80 days | 0.10% | 2.76 Earth radii | Super-Earth / Mini-Neptune |
| STAC-3 b | 37.5 days* | ~0.09% | 1.64 Earth radii | Super-Earth |

*The STAC-3 period is uncertain because its observed transit pattern is inconsistent.*

### Major Discovery: STAC-3's Inconsistent Data

STAC-3 exhibits the most unusual observations among the three systems.

**Observed inconsistencies:**
- Transit depths vary instead of remaining approximately constant.
- An additional dip occurs around day 170.
- The expected dip around day 180 is absent.
- The observed timing does not fit a simple, consistent 40-day orbital period.

### Possible Explanations

The report considers several possible explanations:

1. **A shorter orbital period:** A period of approximately 37.5 days could cause transit timings to drift relative to the observation schedule.
2. **Transit geometry:** A planet crossing closer to the edge of the stellar disc may block less light, potentially explaining changes in transit depth.
3. **Measurement noise or stellar activity:** These could contribute to irregular brightness measurements, although the report notes that they do not necessarily explain the extra dip.

These are possible interpretations, not confirmed causes. Additional observations are needed to determine which explanation best fits the data.

### Habitability Analysis

STAC-3 b is selected in the report as the preferred candidate for further investigation because it is the closest of the three candidates to its estimated habitable zone in relative terms and has a radius consistent with a possible rocky planet.

However, its orbit is still inside the report's simplified habitable-zone reference distance. The available observations do not establish whether the planet is habitable.

Further investigation would require evidence about its atmosphere, magnetic field, water availability, and actual surface conditions.

**Conclusion:** STAC-3 b is a promising candidate for follow-up analysis, but its irregular transit data introduce uncertainty into its orbital estimates and interpretation.

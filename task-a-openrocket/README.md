# Task A — OpenRocket

## Objective

Design and simulate a two-stage sounding rocket capable of carrying a 1.0 kg scientific payload to a target apogee of 4,000 m on Mustafar, accounting for its unusual atmospheric conditions.

## Key Discoveries

Simulated apogee: 4,052 m

Maximum velocity: 174 m/s

Time to apogee: 42.5 seconds

Descent velocity: 6.04 m/s

Payload capacity: 1.0 kg

Rocket configuration: Two-stage propulsion system

Static stability margin: +0.131 calibres

## Rocket Configuration

The simulated rocket uses:

Stage 1: H73-6 booster motor

Stage 2: I218-14 sustainer motor

Simulation software: OpenRocket

The vehicle is 54.5 cm long, with a maximum diameter of 5 cm.

## Environmental Analysis

The simulation incorporates Mustafar's specified environmental conditions:

Surface gravity: 6.50 m/s²

Atmospheric pressure: 75 kPa

Surface temperature: 700 K

Atmospheric density: 0.374 kg/m³

Wind velocity: 10 m/s

## Earth vs. Mustafar Simulation

Parameter|Earth-Standard Model|Mustafar Model
|--------|--------------------|--------------
|Apogee  | 1,820 m            | 4,052 m
Maximum velocity|132 m/s| 174 m/s
Stability margin| +0.21 cal| +0.131 cal
Descent rate| 4.15 m/s| 6.04 m/s
Downrange drift| 45 m| 10 m

## Major Findings

The simulation indicates that lower gravity and reduced atmospheric density substantially improve the rocket's altitude and peak velocity under the specified conditions.

However, the thinner atmosphere also reduces parachute braking effectiveness, requiring careful recovery-system optimization. The simulation achieved the target altitude range while maintaining positive static stability and controlled descent.
